# Al Maryah Island: Zoning, Master Plan & Investment Rationale

Al Maryah Island sits on a 117-hectare reclaimed landmass connected to Abu Dhabi’s mainland by two bridges. It was conceived as a single, integrated financial and lifestyle district from the ground up. The master plan divides the island into four functional zones, each with distinct regulatory and investment characteristics.

## Freehold Zones and Ownership Eligibility

Three of the four zones are designated for freehold ownership by UAE nationals and GCC citizens. Non-GCC foreign buyers can purchase freehold property in specific sub-areas within the island’s investment zone, primarily concentrated around the Galleria Mall, the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) precinct, and the residential towers along the eastern waterfront. The fourth zone, which includes the Cleveland Clinic and certain government facilities, remains leasehold only.

The freehold boundaries are legally defined by Law No. 19 of 2017 and subsequent amendments. Title deeds issued for Al Maryah Island properties are registered with the Abu Dhabi Real Estate Centre (ADREC), not the Dubai Land Department, a distinction that matters for mortgage processes and inheritance rules. Foreign buyers should verify that a specific development plot falls within the gazetted freehold zone before signing any reservation agreement.

## Infrastructure as an Investment Thesis

The island's infrastructure was built to a density and specification that most Abu Dhabi residential districts lack. The district cooling plant, the underground service tunnels, and the 1.2 km of retail frontage along the Galleria were all constructed before the first residential tower topped out. This sequencing is unusual in off-plan markets, typically infrastructure lags occupancy. On Al Maryah Island, the reverse holds.

Three infrastructure anchors shape property values directly:

**Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM)** operates as a common law financial free zone with its own civil and commercial courts. As of 2024, ADGM houses over 1,900 registered entities, including global asset managers, insurance firms, and fintech companies. The executive and professional workforce these firms bring creates a tenant pool with high rent tolerance and long lease durations. Residential units within walking distance of ADGM command a measurable rent premium over comparable units in Reem Island or Al Raha Beach.

**Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi** is a 490-bed multispecialty hospital that functions as a regional referral center. Its presence stabilizes the island's demographic mix, medical professionals, long-term patients, and their families form a consistent rental demand segment that is less sensitive to tourism cycles. The hospital employs roughly 3,000 staff, many of whom prefer housing within a 15-minute commute.

**The Galleria Mall** provides 130+ retail outlets and a dining promenade. It is not a tourist mall, its tenant mix (Waitrose, Borders, a multiplex cinema) targets resident rather than visitor spending. This matters for capital appreciation because it reduces the island's vacancy risk during tourism downturns. A residential district with a functioning grocery-anchored retail core holds value differently than one dependent on boutique pop-ups.

## Economic Drivers and Value Underpinning

The island's investment rationale rests on three overlapping demand layers rather than a single narrative.

First, ADGM's continued expansion creates organic absorption. Each new financial license issued increases the pool of professionals who need housing within a walkable commute. ADGM's regulatory framework actively encourages physical presence, virtual licenses are limited, which translates into real occupancy demand.

Second, the island benefits from Abu Dhabi's sovereign wealth strategy. ADQ, Mubadala, and the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority collectively manage assets exceeding USD 1 trillion. Senior employees of these entities, plus the international banks and law firms that service them, form a high-income demographic that Al Maryah Island's luxury residential stock targets directly. This is not a market of speculators flipping units, it is a market of salaried professionals with rental budgets of AED 200,000, 500,000 per year.

Third, the supply constraint is structural. Al Maryah Island has no undeveloped land parcels available for new residential construction. Every square meter is allocated. Once the remaining plots are built out, approximately 2,000, 3,000 additional residential units expected by 2028, the island reaches full build-out. After that, no new supply enters the market. In a city where new districts regularly add 10,000+ units in a cycle, this fixed supply ceiling is the strongest single argument for long-term capital preservation.

## A Caveat on Liquidity

The same supply constraint that supports value growth also depresses transaction velocity. Al Maryah Island properties trade less frequently than comparable units on Saadiyat Island or Yas Island. Resale listings can sit on the market for 6 to 12 months at asking price. Investors who need to exit quickly should price their units at a discount or accept extended marketing periods. This is a market for hold periods measured in years, not 12-month flips.

For buyers evaluating off-plan opportunities here, the relevant comparison is not against Dubai Marina or Downtown Dubai. It is against other sovereign-adjacent financial districts globally, Canary Wharf in London, La Défense in Paris, or the Marina Bay area in Singapore. Those comparisons suggest that Al Maryah Island's current per-square-foot prices (typically AED 1,400, 2,200 for completed units) are not obviously overvalued given the infrastructure density and the demand base.
