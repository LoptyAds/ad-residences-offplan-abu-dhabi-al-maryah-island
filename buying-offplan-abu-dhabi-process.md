# Buying Off-Plan Properties in Abu Dhabi: Process & Due Diligence

This process is not a single transaction. It is a sequence of discrete stages, each with its own legal checkpoint, financial trigger, and risk profile. Treating it as a lump-sum "buy a house" event is how people lose their position.

The workflow below covers the standard off-plan purchase cycle in Abu Dhabi, from the moment you identify a unit through to the handover certificate. The two agencies that govern this are the Abu Dhabi Department of Municipalities and Transport (DMT) and the Abu Dhabi Real Estate Centre (ADREC), which operates the off-plan registry system known as Oqood.

### Stage 1: Reservation Form & Booking Fee

You do not buy a property at this stage. You buy a right of first refusal for a specific unit.

The developer issues a reservation form that names the unit, the agreed price, and the payment plan. You pay a booking fee, typically 5% of the purchase price or a fixed amount (AED 50,000 to AED 100,000 is common). This fee is held by the developer's trust account, not their operating account.

**Due diligence checkpoint:** Verify that the developer's escrow account number is listed on the ADREC website. If it is not, the developer is not legally permitted to sell off-plan units in Abu Dhabi. Do not proceed.

### Stage 2: Sales & Purchase Agreement (SPA) Review

The SPA is the binding contract. In Abu Dhabi, the DMT publishes a standard-form SPA that most reputable developers use. Deviations from this template are red flags.

Key clauses to inspect:

- **Handover date.** It will be stated as a quarter and year (e.g., Q4 2026). Many SPAs include a grace period clause allowing the developer a 6-12 month delay without penalty. This is standard, but the exact number of days matters.
- **Payment plan triggers.** Payments should be linked to construction milestones, not calendar dates. "10% upon completion of slab level 10" is enforceable. "10% on 1 January 2026" is not, unless the building is already at that stage.
- **Default and remedies.** What happens if you miss a payment? The developer can cancel the contract and keep a portion of what you have paid. The maximum retention is typically 30-40% of the total price paid, but this varies. Read the liquidated damages clause.

**Due diligence checkpoint:** Run the developer's commercial license number through the ADREC portal to confirm they have a valid off-plan sales permit for this specific project. A developer can be licensed generally but not permitted to sell a particular tower.

### Stage 3: Oqood Registration

This is the single most important legal step. Oqood is the Abu Dhabi off-plan property registry. It is not optional.

Within 30 days of signing the SPA, the developer must register the contract with ADREC through the Oqood system. You will receive an Oqood certificate with a unique reference number. This certificate proves that you are the legal owner of that specific unit in that specific building, even though the building does not exist yet.

Without an Oqood registration, you are an unsecured creditor of the developer. If the developer goes bankrupt, you have no claim to the unit. You just have a contract claim for your money back, which ranks behind secured lenders.

**Due diligence checkpoint:** Do not accept a screenshot or PDF from the developer. Log into the ADREC Oqood portal yourself (or ask your lawyer to do it) and verify the certificate number is live in the system.

### Stage 4: Payment Plan Execution

Abu Dhabi off-plan payment plans typically fall into two structures:

- **Construction-linked.** Payments are triggered by verified construction progress reports submitted by the consultant engineer to the bank. This is the safer structure because your money releases only when physical work is confirmed.
- **Time-linked.** Payments are due on fixed dates regardless of construction progress. This structure is riskier. If the developer stalls at 30% completion, you still owe the next payment on the scheduled date.

Most Abu Dhabi developers now use a 60/40 or 50/50 split: 50% during construction, 50% on handover. Some offer 80/20, but those usually carry a higher per-square-foot price.

**Practical note:** Your payments go into the escrow account held by a UAE bank. The developer can only draw from that account upon presenting a certificate from the project's supervising engineer confirming the milestone is met. The bank does not release funds on the developer's say-so alone.

### Stage 5: Handover & Title Deed

When the building receives its completion certificate from the DMT, the developer will invite you for a snagging inspection. You have the right to inspect the unit and list defects. The developer is obligated to fix snags within a reasonable period, typically 30-90 days depending on the severity.

After snags are resolved (or waived), you pay the final installment. The developer then transfers the property from the Oqood registry to the permanent title deed registry. You receive a Title Deed from the DMT. This is the document that proves freehold ownership.

**Due diligence checkpoint:** The developer must also provide a No Objection Certificate from the building's main contractor confirming all contractor dues are paid. Without this, you could inherit a lien on the property.

### Developer Verification: The Minimum Viable Check

If you are working with an advisor, they should be able to provide the following without being asked:

1. Developer's ADREC off-plan sales permit number.
2. Escrow account details and bank name.
3. At least two completed projects by the same developer in Abu Dhabi, with handover dates that matched the SPA (or were within the grace period).
4. The developer's paid-up capital as listed on their trade license. A developer with AED 5 million in capital building a AED 500 million tower is a different risk profile than one with AED 200 million in capital.
