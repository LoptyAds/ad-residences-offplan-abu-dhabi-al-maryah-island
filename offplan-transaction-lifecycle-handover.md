# Transaction Lifecycle: From Off-Plan Booking to Handover

The off-plan transaction on Al Maryah Island is not a single purchase event. It is a staged financial and legal process that runs from initial booking through to the issuance of the final title deed. For a buyer, the critical skill is not just picking the right unit, but managing the cash flow and legal checkpoints across an 18-to-48-month timeline.

## Booking & Deposit

Once a unit is selected from the developer’s inventory, the buyer signs a Reservation Form and pays a booking deposit. In Abu Dhabi, this deposit is typically 5% of the purchase price. It secures the unit and triggers the preparation of the Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA).

The SPA is not a standard form. Every developer files a master community declaration, but the specific payment schedule, handover date, and penalty clauses for delay are negotiated into this individual contract. The Abu Dhabi Department of Municipalities and Transport (DMT) requires all SPAs to be registered via the TAMM portal or through the developer’s approved system. A buyer should never transfer the deposit without seeing the final SPA terms.

## Milestone Payment Schedule

Off-plan payments in Abu Dhabi are tied to construction milestones, not calendar months. A typical schedule for a mid-to-high-end tower on Al Maryah Island might look like this:

- 5% booking deposit
- 10% on SPA signing
- 10% on foundation completion
- 10% on 10th floor slab
- 10% on 20th floor slab
- 10% on roof completion
- 10% on plastering
- 10% on MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) fit-out
- 10% on finishing
- 15% on handover

The exact percentages vary by developer. Some demand 20% at handover. Others front-load to 40% before the building reaches halfway. The buyer’s obligation is to verify each milestone before releasing payment. An independent engineer’s report costs roughly 2,000, 5,000 AED and is worth every dirham if a developer claims “foundation complete” when only the excavation is done.

## Construction Progress Monitoring

This is where most first-time buyers drop the ball. The developer sends a milestone invoice. The buyer pays. No one inspects.

Visit the site monthly. Take dated photos from the same vantage point. Compare them against the developer’s published construction schedule.

One honest caveat: construction delays are common. A 2022 analysis of Abu Dhabi off-plan projects showed that roughly 30% of towers hand over 6 to 12 months late. The SPA should include a penalty clause (liquidated damages) that compensates the buyer per day of delay. If the developer refuses to include one, factor that risk into your price negotiation.

## Snagging & Inspection

The developer issues a Notice of Handover when the unit is substantially complete. “Substantially complete” does not mean perfect. It means the unit is habitable and the main services are connected.

The buyer has a statutory right to inspect the unit before accepting keys. This is the snagging phase. Hire a professional snagging inspector who uses thermal imaging for insulation gaps, moisture meters for plumbing leaks, and a level for door and window alignment. A typical snagging report for a new Abu Dhabi apartment lists 15 to 40 defects. Common items:

- Poorly sealed window frames (causes dust ingress)
- Uneven tile grouting
- Inconsistent AC airflow in the farthest room
- Scratched kitchen countertops
- Missing or misaligned electrical socket covers

Do not sign the handover certificate until the snagging report is delivered to the developer and a timeline for repairs is agreed in writing. The developer is legally obligated to fix structural defects for 10 years under Abu Dhabi law. Cosmetic defects typically have a 1-year warranty.

## Final Title Transfer

The last step is the legal transfer of ownership. The buyer pays the remaining balance (the final milestone). The developer issues a No Objection Certificate (NOC) confirming all service charges and utility dues are cleared. The buyer then registers the property at the Abu Dhabi Real Estate Centre (ADREC) or through the TAMM portal.

The registration fee is 2% of the purchase price plus a small administrative charge. The buyer receives the Title Deed (Tasleek). At this point, the off-plan transaction is complete. The buyer is the legal owner.

One detail that surprises many buyers: the developer holds the original SPA and all payment receipts during construction. You do not get the Title Deed until the final payment clears. Always request certified copies of each payment receipt from the developer’s accounting department and keep them in a separate file. If the developer goes into liquidation before handover, those receipts are your primary evidence of payment.
