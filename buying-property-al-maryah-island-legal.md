# Buying Property on Al Maryah Island: Eligibility & Legal Framework

## Eligibility: Who Can Buy on Al Maryah Island

Al Maryah Island sits within Abu Dhabi's designated freehold zones. Foreign nationals and GCC citizens are permitted to buy freehold property here, with no requirement for UAE residency or a specific visa category at the point of purchase. The distinction matters: freehold means full ownership of both the structure and the land plot it sits on, not a leasehold arrangement with a fixed expiration.

The Abu Dhabi Law No. 19 of 2005 and its subsequent amendments govern foreign ownership rights. Under this framework, non-UAE nationals can acquire freehold title to property in specified investment zones. Al Maryah Island is one of those zones. GCC nationals enjoy the same rights as UAE citizens in these areas.

A practical constraint: while there is no minimum property value set by law for eligibility, individual developers and specific projects may impose their own thresholds. A buyer should verify the developer's registration with the Abu Dhabi Real Estate Centre (ADREC) and confirm the project sits inside a designated investment area before signing anything.

## Freehold Ownership Rights and Restrictions

Freehold ownership on Al Maryah Island grants the buyer indefinite ownership of the land and building. The owner may sell, lease, mortgage, or bequeath the property. There is no time limit on the ownership period.

Two categories of ownership exist for foreign buyers:

- **Freehold (Tamtlek)** -- Full ownership of the land and building. This is the standard for villas and land plots in designated zones.
- **Usufruct (Mana'fi)** -- Ownership of the building only, on leased land, typically for a fixed term. Some apartment towers on the island operate under this structure.

The difference is not academic. A usufruct property cannot be sold as freehold, and the land reverts to the original owner when the term expires. Most off-plan apartments marketed as "freehold" on Al Maryah Island are actually freehold in the building structure, but the land beneath may be government-owned with a long-term lease. The title deed will reflect this.

## Title Deed Registration Process

The Abu Dhabi Real Estate Centre (ADREC) maintains the official property registry. Every sale must be registered here to be legally enforceable. The process:

1. **Memorandum of Understanding (MoU)** -- Buyer and seller sign a preliminary agreement outlining the price, payment schedule, and handover date.
2. **No Objection Certificate (NOC)** -- The developer or master community issues an NOC confirming no outstanding fees or restrictions on the property.
3. **ADREC submission** -- Both parties (or their authorized representatives) submit the MoU, NOC, passport copies, and proof of payment to ADREC.
4. **Title deed issuance** -- ADREC registers the transfer and issues a digital title deed. The buyer receives a unique property registration number.

Fees: ADREC charges 2% of the property value as a registration fee, plus a small administrative fee (typically AED 500-1,000). Off-plan purchases have a different registration timeline -- the title deed is issued only after the property is completed and the developer obtains the completion certificate.

## Developer Regulations Specific to Al Maryah Island

Developers on Al Maryah Island operate under the oversight of the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority (ADIA) and the Mubadala Investment Company, which co-own the master development. This means stricter financial vetting than projects in other Abu Dhabi zones.

Key developer obligations:

- **Escrow account requirement** -- All off-plan sales proceeds must go into a regulated escrow account. ADREC releases funds to the developer in stages, tied to construction milestones. A buyer should verify the escrow account number and the supervising bank before transferring any deposit.
- **Completion guarantee** -- Some developers on the island offer a bank guarantee or insurance policy covering project completion. This is not legally required but is common for premium projects.
- **Strata law compliance** -- Al Maryah Island follows Abu Dhabi's strata ownership law (Law No. 3 of 2012). Owners in multi-unit buildings hold a share of the common areas proportional to their unit size. Service charges are set by the developer or a designated management company, subject to ADREC approval.

One limitation worth noting: the island's master plan restricts certain property types. Commercial use within residential buildings is tightly controlled. A buyer planning to operate a short-term rental or a home business should check the specific building's community rules, which may be more restrictive than Abu Dhabi's general regulations.

## Practical Takeaway

For a buyer considering a property on Al Maryah Island, the legal framework is stable and foreigner-friendly, but the details of ownership structure (freehold vs. usufruct) and the specific developer's escrow compliance are where most problems surface. ADREC's registry is public and searchable. A buyer can verify a developer's registration status and any encumbrances on a specific property by requesting the property's "title deed extract" from ADREC directly.
